Voluntary Disclosure and Investment Inefficiency

Shibao Liu, Somnath Das and Rustam Zufarov

Abstract

This paper examines the effect of voluntary disclosure on corporate investment efficiency. The role of voluntary disclosure in attenuating information asymmetries in capital markets is well documented, but whether a firm's investment choices, and particularly its investment efficiency, are affected by managers' voluntary disclosures is less well understood. We document that voluntary disclosure is negatively associated with under-investment and positively associated with over-investment. Results are consistent across several measures of voluntary disclosure and robust to an extensive set of controls. A staggered difference-in-differences design based on firms' inclusion in the S&P 500 index suggests the results are less likely to be driven by endogeneity. Managers' empire-building and propensity to make risky investments exacerbate the positive association between disclosure and over-investment, while the effect is mitigated for firms that operate in multiple segments, face stronger competition, hold more proprietary information, or employ higher-ability managers.
