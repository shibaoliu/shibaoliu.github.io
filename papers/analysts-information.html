An Empirical Examination of the Role of Analysts' Information in Managers' Voluntary Disclosures

Shibao Liu and Somnath Das

Under review at The Accounting Review

Abstract

This paper examines whether and how analysts’ information influences managers’ voluntary disclosure. Our primary focus is on the component of analyst information ‘unavailable to managers’. Our primary proxy for this construct uses the Barron, Kim, Lim, and Stevens (1998) measure of analysts’ private information. We document that as the amount of information unavailable to managers increases, so does voluntary disclosure. Our results are robust to alternative measures, alternative estimation approaches, and quasi-exogenous variation from brokerage mergers and closures. Additional analysis suggests that this effect is in part driven by the component of macroeconomic information and local news included in analyst forecasts. Furthermore, the effect is stronger for larger firms and firms with higher demand from analysts and weaker for firms with higher costs of voluntary disclosure. Overall, we document that analysts’ information influences managers’ voluntary disclosures. This result is consistent with the notion that managers do learn from analysts.
